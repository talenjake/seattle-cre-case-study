# Seattle Multifamily Acquisition Case Study

## Lake City Apartments
**2600 NE 125th St, Seattle, Washington**

This case study evaluates a hypothetical acquisition of Lake City Apartments using public listing information, Seattle multifamily market data, recent transaction references, and clearly disclosed analyst assumptions.

| Equity Multiple | 1.57x |

## Executive View

Lake City Apartments is an eight-unit Seattle multifamily property analyzed at a $1.50 million asking price. The underwriting begins with the public listing economics and does not assume an aggressive immediate mark-to-market rent increase.

Under the five-year base case, the property produces a 10.1% levered IRR and 1.57x equity multiple. Initial cash yield is modest, so the investment thesis depends more heavily on durable occupancy, moderate NOI growth, debt amortization, and a disciplined exit.

## Investment Thesis

- The 6.08% in-place cap rate is above the Q2 2026 Seattle market average used in the analysis.
- The $187,500 per-unit asking basis is below two recent small Seattle apartment transactions reviewed.
- Recent roof and electrical improvements reduce some near-term capital uncertainty.
- The base case avoids relying on a large immediate rent mark-to-market.
- The property provides direct exposure to Seattle multifamily demand at a relatively low acquisition basis.

## Key Risks

- Initial levered cash-on-cash return is modest at the assumed cost of debt.
- Eight units create tenant concentration and limited operating scale.
- Citywide rent averages are market context rather than direct Lake City rent comparables.
- Returns remain sensitive to exit cap-rate expansion.
- Property-level due diligence could reveal operating or capital needs not visible in public data.

## Return Analysis

The base case produces:

- **1.30x Year 1 DSCR**
- **3.4% Year 1 cash-on-cash return**
- **10.1% five-year levered IRR**
- **1.57x equity multiple**

The primary weakness is financing. At the assumed 6.0% cost of debt, the initial cash yield is not especially high. The return profile therefore depends on moderate operating growth, loan amortization, and exit proceeds rather than aggressive short-term value creation.

## Methodology

Public listing metrics are used where available. Because the publicly accessible listing does not provide a complete rent roll or detailed operating statement, gross potential rent and operating expenses are derived from the stated GRM, occupancy, and cap rate.

Debt terms, acquisition costs, growth rates, capital reserves, selling costs, hold period, and exit cap rate are analyst assumptions created for this educational underwriting exercise.
